Course Goals


1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

These projects helped me consider technology’s role in acts of making by showing me how code can function as a technical tool and a creative medium. Through ideas like numerical representation, modularity, automation, variability, and transcoding, I learned how digital media is created, organized, and transformed. For example, modularity allowed me to build projects from separate elements, and variability showed me how the same project could look different depending on the code, content, or how I interacted with it. It was especially interesting in how changing a few numerical values in the code could completely transform the appearance or behavior of a project. A prime example of this was our Glitching Images assignment, where we could directly see how code shapes the visual output of a digital image. By changing the image’s code, we were able to alter its appearance. This shows how closely code and design are connected, as even small changes to numerical values could produce an entirely different artistic result.

2. Embrace experimentation

I embraced experimentation throughout each of these projects. As a Marketing major with limited coding experience, I had to become comfortable with trying new approaches and learning through trial and error. During our first project, the Code and Technology Narrative, I was unfamiliar with how code could be used as part of the design process. Even when I followed the instructions carefully, I still had to test different options, identify and hopefully resolve any problems, and revise my work. I leaned even further into experimentation during the New Media Webtext Experiment because we had more creative freedom to explore what “new media” could look like. I tested different code blocks, looked up solutions when I encountered issues, and applied new commands. Through this, I created a somewhat interactive grocery store–inspired website. These experiences showed me that experimentation is an essential part of the process.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

I found our workshop classes especially valuable because they allowed me to see what my classmates were creating and learn from their work. Since I came into the course with limited coding experience, I appreciated being surrounded by classmates with higheer levels of coding knowledge and skill. I was able to ask how they created certain features, compare the challenges we encountered, and work together to find possible solutions. The workshop environment made the learning process feel more collaborative and I learned that asking questions and exchanging ideas can strengthen both the process and the final project.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Revision was a constant part of my work throughout these projects. I continue to revisit earlier assignments as I learn new coding techniques and discover ways to strengthen them. Although revision is often viewed as something that happens after a project is completed, these projects taught me to revise throughout the creative process. When a section of code did not work as intended, I had to troubleshoot and adjust it immediately. Because the code shaped the appearance and function of each project, revising the code also meant revising the design. It's so interesting think about how rearranging a few lines of code, numerical values, changes an entire visual output artistically. This process helped me understand revision as an ongoing interaction between technical choices, artistic decisions, and the digital output.

5. Learn to present your work

Presenting my projects in small workshop groups and occasionally to the entire class helped me become more confident in sharing my work. Even when I was not completely satisfied with a project because the code did not function as intended or certain elements appeared incorrectly on the published site, I learned to still present it openly. These experiences helped me become more comfortable discussing my creative decisions and the challenges I faced. I also learned that presenting doesn't mean my project needs to be perfect; it's an opportunity to explain my process, receive feedback, and identify ways to improve.

Photo Essay

A photo essay about taking the time to slow down, take detours, and enjoy unexpected moments.

Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

This unit made me think more about the technology behind the things we create. At the most basic level, digital photos, audio, and video are represented by code and numbers, yet we can shape them into something expressive and personal. Using Audacity, Audition, Lightroom, InDesign, and Premiere Pro helped me see how technology influences both the creative process and the final result.

For the Sound Composition project, I recorded sounds on my phone and transferred them to Audacity. From there, I could cut, trim, and rearrange separate recordings into one cohesive composition. The software allowed me to take everyday sounds from a trip to the store and give them a different rhythm and meaning through editing.

For the Exploratory Podcast, I recorded my voice on my phone using the microphone on my wired headphones, then imported the recording into Audition. I used this technology to rearrange clips, add music, and adjust how the music faded in and out. Working with both Audacity and Audition showed me how different programs can support similar creative outputs while having different approaches. Learning each program’s tools also influenced the choices I made as I edited.

For the Photo/Text Collage, I used InDesign to bring photos and text together. Although I had used the program before, this project gave me another way to explore its tools. Changing fonts, adjusting text sizes, and moving elements around affected how the words and images related to one another and where the viewer’s attention went.

For the Photo Essay, I combined Lightroom and Premiere Pro. In Lightroom, I adjusted the lighting in my photos, which showed me how editing could change the mood of an image. In Premiere Pro, I arranged the photos, added and positioned text, chose music, and adjusted transitions and the amount of time each image stayed on screen. These technologies helped me turn still photos into a story/journey with movement and pacing. Using multiple programs also showed me how different technologies can work together.


2. Embrace experimentation

These projects helped me become more comfortable with experimentation, especially because so much of the process involved learning unfamiliar programs. I came in with experience using InDesign from another class, but I had never used Audacity, Audition, or Premiere Pro. Each platform had a learning curve, so experimenting with the tools became part of figuring out how to bring my ideas to life.

The Sound Composition project was my first time using Audacity. I recorded sound clips during a trip to and from the store, then experimented with their order while editing. I moved the clips around to hear which sounds flowed naturally together and which created interesting contrasts. Trying different arrangements helped me find a sequence I liked.

For the Exploratory Podcast project, I used Audition for the first time. It was harder for me to figure out, but I’m glad I worked through it because I learned a lot. Much of my experimentation involved deciding where to place music throughout the podcast. I tried a version without music, one with music only at the beginning and end, and another with music interwoven throughout. Comparing these versions helped me hear how music affected the flow and mood of the podcast and decide what worked best.

With the Photo/Text Collage, I admittedly had absolutely no ideas on what I wanted to create, so experimentation helped me explore possible directions. Although I had used InDesign in another class, this project pushed me to try different layouts and combinations of photos and text. I don’t love how the final piece turned out, but the process gave me room to try ideas without knowing exactly where they would lead.

I especially enjoyed the Photo Essay project because I love making short-form videos, even when the visuals are still photos, like in this project. I experimented with fonts, text colors, and placement to make the video feel playful and visually engaging. Sometimes I placed text sideways, lined it up with the railing of a bridge, or positioned it at the top or bottom of a photo to draw the viewer’s eye to different parts of the screen. I also tried different photo selections and sequences to find an order that best captured the journey I took that day. This project gave me my first experience with Premiere Pro, a program I had always wanted to learn. Experimenting with transitions and fading the music in and out helped me see how small editing choices could make the whole piece flow more smoothly.


3. Engage with others in a workshop environment

Just like in the last unit, I loved our workshop classes because they gave us a chance to see each other’s work and help one another work through problems. Seeing my classmates’ projects also gave me ideas for things I could try in the future. Changing workshop groups helped me meet new people and learn more about their creative approaches.

During the Sound Composition workshop, we shared what we liked about each other’s pieces. I especially remember liking the glitch-like sound Mira added to her composition. It made me think about how different effects could make a sound more interesting or unexpected.

For the Exploratory Podcast workshop, I enjoyed hearing the topics my classmates explored and the different ways they used music and sound effects. One classmate included the sound of someone walking in high heels, which made me feel like I was right there in the scene. It was like audible imagery. Hearing that encouraged me to consider how sound effects could make my own podcast more immersive.

In the Photo/Text Collage workshop, I liked seeing how everyone approached the project differently and chose different quotes from the reading. Even though we were working with the same text, each collage offered a different visual interpretation. It helped me see possibilities I might not have considered on my own.

For the Photo Essay workshop, I loved seeing the photos my classmates took and hearing the music they paired with them. Each video felt personal, and it was fun to get a glimpse into their days through the moments they chose to share.


4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Revision has been a constant part of these projects, and I’m still returning to earlier assignments as I learn new things. I usually think of revision as something that happens after a piece is finished, but these projects helped me see how much it happens throughout the creative process. It was also interesting to see what revision looks like across audio, photography, and video, using programs such as Audacity, Audition, Lightroom, and Premiere Pro.

For the Sound Composition project, I constantly revised the order of the sound clips, where each clip started and ended, and which sounds to include. I tried several versions before settling on the arrangement that I thought sounded the most engaging.

For the Exploratory Podcast, I made a more substantial revision after listening to my first exported version. I had only included music at the beginning and end, and the middle felt flat when I listened back. I returned to the project and added music throughout to give it more energy and support the flow of the discussion. Looking back now, I’d like to revisit it again and possibly add sound effects.

For the Photo/Text Collage, revision was part of figuring out what I wanted to create. I felt uninspired and struggled to find a clear direction, so I kept adjusting the layout, colors, and placement of the images and text. In this case, revising helped me explore ideas as I worked, even when I wasn’t sure what the final result should look like.

For the Photo Essay, revision also involved troubleshooting technical problems. When I exported the video, the ending would lag and then suddenly speed up. I had to return to the project, make adjustments, and export it again to check whether the issue was resolved. This showed me that revision can involve both creative choices and technical changes that help a piece work the way I intended.


5. Learn to present your work

This unit helped me feel more comfortable sharing my work. Presenting in small groups first helped me build the confidence to share my Exploratory Podcast with the entire class. That pushed me outside my comfort zone because I don’t like hearing my voice on recording, especially in front of other people! Although it was uncomfortable at first, the experience helped me get more used to hearing myself and feel more confident presenting something I created.
